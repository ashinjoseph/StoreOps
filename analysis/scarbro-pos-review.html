What the POS database actually says

A read of 17,718 transactions from Scarbro Mart's point-of-sale backup. The headline finding is that the revenue number the system reports is not the revenue the store earns — and two data gaps decide what else can honestly be measured.

Lottery is 40% of turnover and almost none of the income

The POS totals every lottery ticket as a sale. But ticket money belongs to OLG — the store books a commission on it, not the face value. Separating the two changes the size of the business considerably.

Fix at the till Three cash entries corrupt every payment report

Three transactions have a mis-keyed cash tendered amount — $804,906,004,014 taken for a $20.00 sale, $82,000,796,855 for $10.26, and $5,660,300 for $30.51. They push reported cash to $886.9 billion. Any report that sums the cash column is wrong until these are corrected.

The good news: the error sits only in the tender field. GrandTotal is untouched on all three, so sales figures were never affected — and every figure on this page excludes them.

Blocks profit reporting Cost price is missing on 94% of what you sell

Only 102 of the 842 products sold carry a purchase cost — covering 6.8% of revenue. The POS still prints a margin column, and it currently sums to $213,508, roughly 90% of sales. That is not your margin; it is what the calculation returns when cost defaults to zero.

So there is no profit analysis on this page. Everything here is revenue and volume, which the data supports. Fill in PurchaseCost for your top ~100 sellers and real margin reporting becomes possible immediately — those items already cover most of your turnover.

What to do with this

  1. Correct the three cash entries.

    Invoices 13424, 6795 and 17. Set the tendered amount to something sane and the payment reports stop being nonsense. Then add a till-side sanity check — reject any tender more than, say, $500 over the sale total.

  2. Stop reporting lottery as revenue.

    Split it out everywhere, in the POS and in StoreOps. Track it as tickets sold, payouts made, and commission earned. Otherwise the store looks like a $237K business when the retail side is $143K.

  3. Backfill cost on your top 100 products.

    They already account for the large majority of revenue. This is the single change that unlocks margin reporting — and margin, not revenue, is what tells you whether beer or tobacco is actually worth the shelf space.

  4. Use the hourly map for scheduling.

    The busy windows are consistent week to week, so cover is a planning decision rather than a guess.
