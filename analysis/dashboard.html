Read the drinks growth with care

The window runs mid-April to mid-August, so it goes into summer and stops. Drinks, beer and ready-to-drink rising over exactly that stretch is what seasonality looks like as well as what growth looks like, and four months cannot tell the two apart. Treat those as seasonal until a full year says otherwise. Tobacco holding flat through its own low season, and the tobacco share loss below, are the more reliable signals.

What to do with this

  1. Treat the shrinking basket as the problem to solve.

  2. Work the lottery queue.

  3. Give drinks and ready-to-drink the space they are earning — but book the decision for review in October.

    Both are up sharply and both are exactly the categories summer inflates. Expanding facings now is reasonable; treating the growth as permanent is not. Re-run this analysis once you have data through autumn and see which of them holds.

  4. Watch tobacco's share, not its total.

  5. Start capturing cost price.

    Cost is missing on 94% of what sells, so none of this can be turned into profit. Revenue tells you what moves; only margin tells you what is worth the shelf. Backfilling the top 100 sellers would cover most of turnover and make the next version of this report a profit report.

Method and limits

Source. RetailPOS_DB, a SQL Server full backup taken 19 Aug 2026, restored and queried directly. Figures come from InvoiceInfo, Invoice_Product and Invoice_Payment, with line items classified by the product master's category.

Repairs applied. Three invoices carried mis-keyed cash-tendered amounts, the worst being $804,906,004,014 against a $20.00 sale, which made every cash total meaningless. Tendered has been set equal to the sale amount on those three. GrandTotal was never affected, so sales figures were correct all along.

Trend method. Ordinary least squares on whole Sun–Sat weeks, reported with R² and a two-sided p-value. "Significant" means p < 0.05. The window holds 17 whole weeks, which is enough to detect a strong trend and not enough to detect a weak one — a "not significant" result here means not proven, not proven flat.

The seasonality limit. Four months running into summer cannot separate trend from season. Any category whose growth would be explained by warm weather should be read as unproven until a full year exists.

No profit figures. Purchase cost is present on 102 of 842 products sold, covering 6.8% of revenue. The POS margin column sums to about 90% of sales, which is what that calculation returns when cost defaults to zero. Nothing on this page uses it.

Privacy. InvoiceInfo holds cardholder fields. None were read. Every figure here is an aggregate.
